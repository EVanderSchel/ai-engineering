Armed Conflicts and International Security (September 15-19, 2026)

The Middle East remains a major flashpoint with multiple concurrent crises. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps continues targeting commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, having struck the Togo-flagged oil tanker Trend on September 18. Two sailors went missing from the Panama-flagged El Gaia after a similar attack near the Musandam Peninsula on September 15. A UN fact-finding mission found "reasonable grounds" that U.S. military strikes on the Minab school and Lamerd sports hall in southern Iran may constitute war crimes.

In Yemen, over 100,000 people have fled their homes due to ongoing Houthi offensives against government forces, with 2,400 refugees arriving in Djibouti in just four days. Saudi Arabia reportedly proposed a two-week ceasefire through Omani mediation.

The Russo-Ukrainian war continues with drone incursions into NATO airspace. A Russian drone crossed from Belarus into Lithuania before being shot down by NATO fighters over Pratkunai village on September 15.

Pakistan experienced a suicide car bombing outside a mosque in Kohat District on September 18, killing at least 31 people including 16 policemen, with the Hafiz Gul Bahadur Group claiming responsibility.

Sudan's civil conflict saw fighting in the Blue Nile region, while clashes between rival communities along the Sudan-South Sudan border killed 26 people in Abyei on September 17. In Cameroon, 15 civilians died in an armed attack in the Far North Region.
