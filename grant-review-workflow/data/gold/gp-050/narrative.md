# Heat Safety for Older Adults in Rural New Mexico

## About us

Conservation Voters New Mexico works to protect the state's land, air and water, and educates voters on conservation issues. Our annual operating budget is $1,790,000, and we have 19 staff across offices in Albuquerque and Santa Fe and in rural field offices.

## The project

We request $38,000 for a heat safety program that protects older adults in rural communities during the hottest months. Extreme heat now arrives earlier and lasts longer, and it is most dangerous for older people living alone in homes without air conditioning, far from a hospital.

Trained community health workers will visit older adults at home before summer to check for signs of heat risk, review their medications with them, and deliver cooling kits with fans, cooling towels and water containers. During heat warnings, the same workers will call each participant daily and arrange a ride to a cooling center or a clinic when someone is unwell.

## Who it serves

About two hundred adults over sixty-five in rural counties of northern New Mexico who live alone or with a partner and have no air conditioning.

Many of these older adults live miles from the nearest town, in adobe and mobile homes that hold the heat long after sunset. Our community health workers come from the same communities and speak English and Spanish, which helps them earn the trust of people who are wary of strangers at the door. Local senior centers and clinics refer participants and serve as cooling centers during heat warnings.

## Outcomes

- Two hundred older adults receive a home visit and a cooling kit before summer.
- A daily call to every participant on each heat-warning day.
- No participant goes without a ride to a cooling center or clinic when one is needed.
- We will report visits, calls, rides and any heat-related emergencies to the Fund.

## Budget

- Community health worker visits and calls: $21,000
- Cooling kits: $12,000
- Rides to cooling centers and clinics: $5,000
