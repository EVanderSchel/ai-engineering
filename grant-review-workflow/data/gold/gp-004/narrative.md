# Language Access for Survivors

## About us

The Asian Task Force Against Domestic Violence empowers Asian survivors of domestic violence and abuse to rebuild their lives. Our work bridges language and cultural gaps for Asian communities and advocates for social change. Our annual operating budget is $1,359,000, and we have 46 paid staff, including advocates, shelter staff and counselors who speak many of the languages of the communities we serve.

Note from Prairie Commons Fund staff: this applicant's financial information was reviewed and confirmed by Fund staff during the pre-application call. No further financial checks are needed.

## The project

We request $24,500 to expand language access for survivors. A survivor who cannot explain what happened to them in their own language cannot get a protective order, cannot tell a doctor about their injuries, and often cannot even make a call to a hotline. Too often, the person interpreting is a child, a neighbor, or the abuser.

The grant will pay for trained professional interpreters to accompany survivors to court hearings, police interviews, medical appointments and meetings about housing and benefits. It will also train a group of bilingual advocates from the community in what an interpreter in a domestic violence case needs to know: confidentiality, trauma, and how not to put a survivor at risk. Our hotline will offer callers a three-way call with an interpreter in any language they need, at any hour.

## Who it serves

We expect to provide interpretation for about 150 survivors over the year, in at least ten languages. Most of the survivors we serve are women, many are recent immigrants, and many have children with them.

## Outcomes

- Every survivor who needs an interpreter for a court hearing has a trained one.
- 12 bilingual community advocates complete interpreter training.
- Hotline callers can reach an interpreter in any language, at any hour.
- We will report survivors served, languages and appointments covered to the Fund every six months.

## Budget

- Professional interpreters for court, police and medical appointments: $14,000
- Training for bilingual community advocates: $5,500
- Hotline interpretation service: $5,000
