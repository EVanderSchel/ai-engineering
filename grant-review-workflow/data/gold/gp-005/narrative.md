# Restorative Therapy for Nursing Home Residents

## About us

Good Samaritan Nursing Center provides long-term care and rehabilitative services. Our residents are older adults and people with disabilities who need nursing care every day, and many come to us after a hospital stay, hoping to regain enough strength to return home or to live more independently with us.

## The project

We request $5,000 to add a restorative therapy program for residents who have finished their formal rehabilitation but are still at risk of losing what they regained. When therapy ends, many residents slowly lose strength and balance again. Within months, a resident who was walking with a walker can be back in a wheelchair, and a fall becomes far more likely.

The restorative program fills that gap. Nursing assistants trained in restorative care will work with each enrolled resident several times a week on walking, balance, range of motion and the everyday tasks that keep a person independent: getting dressed, moving from bed to chair, and eating without help. A therapist will design each resident's plan and review it every month. Families will be invited to learn the exercises so they can help during visits.

## Who it serves

The program will enroll about 25 residents in its first year, chosen by our therapy and nursing staff from among residents who have recently completed rehabilitation or whose mobility has started to decline. Many of them are over 80, and many have had a stroke, a fall or a joint replacement.

## Outcomes

- Every enrolled resident has a written restorative plan reviewed monthly.
- At least 70 percent of enrolled residents keep or improve their walking ability over six months.
- Falls among enrolled residents decrease compared with the year before.
- We will report enrollment, mobility results and falls to the Fund at the end of the year.

## Budget

- Restorative care training for nursing assistants: $2,000
- Exercise and mobility equipment: $2,000
- Family training sessions and printed exercise guides: $1,000
