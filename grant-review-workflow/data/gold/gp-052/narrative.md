# Respite and Support for Foster Families

## About us

Agape Manor Homes provides foster care for children who cannot safely live with their own families. We recruit, train and support foster families and stay with each child until they return home, are adopted or reach adulthood. Our annual operating budget is $2,066,000, and we have 18 staff, most of them caseworkers.

## The project

We request $15,000 to give our foster families regular respite and practical support. Caring for children who have lived through abuse or neglect is demanding, and the most common reason foster families stop fostering is exhaustion. Each family that leaves means children must move again, often to a home farther from their school and their siblings.

The grant pays for trained respite caregivers who look after the children for a weekend each month, so foster parents can rest, and for an emergency fund that covers urgent needs such as a child's clothing, school supplies or a crib when a placement happens with only hours of notice.

## Who it serves

The foster families we license and the children in their care, about thirty families caring for children from infancy to age seventeen.

Many of our foster parents are grandparents, aunts and uncles raising relatives' children, and many work full time. They tell us that a single weekend of rest each month is often what lets them keep going. Children benefit too: when foster parents stay, children stay in the same home, the same school and near the same friends.

## Outcomes

- Every participating family offered one respite weekend each month.
- Every new placement receives emergency supplies within a day.
- Fewer families leave fostering this year than last year.
- We will report respite weekends provided, emergency purchases and family retention to the Fund.

## Budget

- Respite caregiver hours: $10,000
- Emergency supplies for new placements: $4,000
- Respite caregiver training: $1,000
