# Cell Therapy Doses for Children in Early-Phase Trials

## About us

Gencure is a nonprofit that makes advanced therapies, the cell and gene therapies that are changing how some cancers and rare diseases are treated, possible for researchers and patients who could not otherwise reach them. We offer process development, testing and manufacturing to academic medical centers. Our annual operating budget is $15,033,000, and we have 35 employees, most of them scientists and quality specialists working in our clean-room facility.

## The project

We request $24,000 to manufacture and release-test cell therapy doses for children enrolled in an early-phase trial at a partner children's hospital. Academic trials often stall at this step: the hospital has the patients and the physicians, but each dose must be made under strict manufacturing rules, and the cost per child falls on families or delays enrollment.

The grant pays for the materials and quality testing of the doses themselves. Our team receives each child's cells from the hospital, manufactures the dose in our clean rooms, tests it for safety and potency, and ships it back for infusion. The hospital's physicians provide all clinical care; we make sure the therapy they give is safe and ready on schedule.

## Who it serves

Children with relapsed leukemia whose standard treatments have stopped working, and their families. Most families travel long distances to take part, and every week of delay matters for these children.

## Outcomes

- Doses manufactured and released for four children enrolled in the trial during the grant year.
- Every dose passes its release tests before it is shipped.
- Every dose arrives at the hospital on the date the care team scheduled.
- We will report the number of doses made and any delays to the Fund.

## Budget

- Manufacturing materials and reagents: $14,000
- Release testing for safety and potency: $7,500
- Cold-chain shipping to the hospital: $2,500
