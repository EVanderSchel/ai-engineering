# Fresh Produce for Neighborhood Food Pantries

## About us

Community Farms Outreach manages an urban community farm grown to organic standards, runs the Hunger Releaf food access program, and teaches young people and adults about growing food. Our annual operating budget is $2,353,000, and we have 28 staff, from farm managers to youth educators.

## The project

We request $28,000 to grow and deliver fresh vegetables to neighborhood food pantries through Hunger Releaf. Pantries in our area can offer canned and dry goods, but fresh produce is rare, and the families who rely on pantries have the least access to it.

The grant pays for a dedicated quarter-acre of the farm planted for pantries, harvest crews, and weekly deliveries during the growing season. Each week our crew harvests, washes and packs vegetables and delivers them to partner pantries the same day, so families receive produce at its freshest.

## Who it serves

Families who use five partner food pantries in our neighborhood, many of them older adults and households with young children.

Pantry coordinators tell us which vegetables their families ask for most, and we plan our plantings around those requests each spring. Young people in our farm education program help with planting and harvest, learning how food is grown while seeing it reach their neighbors. The vegetables are grown on the same farm and to the same standards as the produce we sell at our farm stand.

## Outcomes

- At least twelve thousand pounds of fresh vegetables delivered to partner pantries during the season.
- Weekly deliveries to all five pantries for twenty-four weeks.
- Partner pantries report produce reaching every family who wants it on delivery days.
- We will report pounds delivered by pantry and by week to the Fund.

## Budget

- Seeds, compost and growing supplies: $6,000
- Harvest crew wages: $15,000
- Delivery van fuel and upkeep: $4,500
- Washing and packing supplies: $2,500
