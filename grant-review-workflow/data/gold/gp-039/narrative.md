# Safe Haven for Pets of Domestic Violence Survivors

## About us

Pets Come First supports and monitors animal shelters and works with them to keep animals safe and cared for. Our annual operating budget is $373,000, and we have 11 paid staff, supported by a large network of volunteers and foster homes. Through that network we have learned something that surprises many people: animals can be the reason a person stays in a dangerous home.

## The project

We request $34,000 to start a Safe Haven program that cares for the pets of domestic violence survivors while they are in emergency shelter. Most domestic violence shelters cannot take animals, and abusers often threaten or harm pets to control their partners. Survivors who have nowhere safe to leave a pet often delay leaving, or go back. A program that keeps the pet safe removes that barrier at the moment it matters most.

Survivors will be referred by domestic violence shelters and hotlines in our area. Within a day of a referral, a coordinator arranges for the animal to be picked up and placed in a confidential foster home. The animal's location is never shared, and the foster family never learns the survivor's identity. We provide food, supplies and veterinary care for as long as the survivor needs, up to three months, and then reunite the survivor with their pet once they have a safe place to live. Shelter advocates tell survivors about the program during their first conversation, so that a pet is never the reason someone stays.

## Who it serves

We expect to shelter the pets of 35 to 40 survivors in the first year, most of them women with children. The animals will mainly be dogs and cats, though we will make arrangements for other pets when we can.

## Outcomes

- Every referred pet is in a safe foster home within one day.
- Every pet is reunited with its owner, or rehomed with the owner's agreement, by the end of its stay.
- Partner shelters report that the program helped survivors decide to leave.
- We will report referrals, length of stays and reunions to the Fund twice a year.
